**In one sentence: this year's market is not simply an "AI rally" — it is the combined result of high rates, high oil and AI spending. In the short run the most important variable is the 10-year Treasury yield; in the long run it makes more sense to prepare for several scenarios than to bet on one outcome.**

## 1. What kind of market this is

**Interest rates anchor the price of every asset, and they are near a 20-year high**. The 10-year Treasury yield is around 5%: it broke above 5% in September, the highest since 2007, and touched 5.36% in October, the highest since 2002. After the midterm results, Treasuries rallied and yields fell — one of the main reasons stocks reversed higher after the vote.

**Oil and inflation have put the Fed back on a hiking path**. The Middle East conflict has kept oil around $90 a barrel, lifting inflation expectations. The Fed raised rates 25 bp to 3.75%–4.00% in September, its first hike since 2023, and markets expect another in December.

**Sectors have diverged sharply**. By early October, energy and technology were the two best S&P 500 sectors, each up about 37% for the year, while health care was up only about 7%. Since late October tech has pulled back hard; the S&P semiconductor index's forward P/E has fallen to 14.7x, below the S&P 500's 18.9x.

Together, the three forces explain the shape of this year's market:

| Force | Lifted | Held back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High rates | Returns on cash and short-term bonds | Richly valued growth stocks, small caps, real estate |
| High oil | Energy stocks | Consumer and transport stocks, and hopes for rate cuts |
| AI capital spending | Semiconductors, power and grid, the data-center supply chain | Cloud providers' own free cash flow |

## 2. Short term (through next spring): signals to watch

**Seasonality favors stocks, but this year adds a hiking cycle**. Historically, the six months after a midterm election (November to April) are the strongest stretch of the four-year presidential cycle, with the S&P 500 up about 14% on average. Those averages do not include a hiking cycle, so they should not be applied mechanically.

The signals most worth watching are below; the dashboard at the top of the Market Watch page updates them every trading day:

1. **10-year Treasury yield**: a move back below 5% would relieve most stocks; a return above 5.3% would make any rally hard to sustain.
2. **Oil**: drives inflation expectations, and with them whether the Fed keeps hiking.
3. **Equal-weight S&P (RSP) vs the S&P 500 (SPY)**: when equal weight leads, gains are spreading to more stocks and a rally is healthier; when only a few giants rise, it is fragile.
4. **Small caps (IWM) vs the S&P 500**: small companies fear high rates most because much of their debt floats. Small caps leading suggests the market believes rates have peaked.
5. **VIX**: how fearful the market is. A spike followed by a fall often comes with a short-term low, but it is not a precise signal.

## 3. Long term (1–5 years): four scenarios

Rather than betting on one outcome, it helps to lay out several, and which assets each favors.

| Scenario | When it happens | Relative winners | Relative losers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AI keeps expanding | Cloud providers' 2027 capex approaches $1 trillion | AI supply-chain bottleneck companies, power and grid | Software and services disrupted by AI |
| Inflation stays high | Oil and wage pressure persist | Energy, real assets, companies with pricing power | Long-term bonds, richly valued growth stocks |
| Growth slows, rates fall | Hikes push the economy down | Long-term Treasuries, health care, consumer staples, small caps once rates fall | Cyclicals, energy |
| AI spending peaks | Cloud providers cut capex | Defensive sectors, cash, bonds | Semiconductors, power, the data-center chain |

A few observations:

- **Energy and the grid are rare sectors that win in two scenarios**: they benefit from both high inflation and AI power demand. But after this year's gains they are no longer cheap.
- **With the 10-year near 5%, long-term Treasuries offer the highest locked-in yield in about 20 years**, and they can still hedge a recession.
- **Health care has clearly lagged this year**; combined with long-term demand from an aging population, it is a defensive sector worth watching.
- **Financials benefit from wider lending margins at high rates**, but the new Congress may tighten regulation, and private credit's exposure to AI data centers deserves attention.

## 4. Main risks

1. **The 10-year yield breaks above 5.5%**: stock valuations would be pushed down across the board, with growth stocks hurt most.
2. **Oil spikes again**: inflation rebounds and the Fed is forced to hike more.
3. **AI capital spending peaks**: tech, power and semiconductors would fall together; they are more correlated than they look.
4. **Trouble in private credit**: a large share of this AI build-out is funded by private credit; defaults would spread to financials.
